# Full quality audit and improvement plan, 11 November 2026

Scope: the published register (`data/public/`), the working database, the live
dashboard at https://esaritas.github.io/Mining-accidents-Turkiye/ in both
languages at 1440x900 and 390x844, the code, the tests, CI and the monitoring
automation. Audited at commit `46154be`.

Every number below was measured during this audit. Where a check could not be
run, that is said. Nothing in the published data, the code or the page was
changed by this audit; this document is its only output.

## 1. Verdict

The published values are internally consistent and the page is technically
sound: it loads in under a second, throws no errors, and every figure on it
agrees with the export. As a piece of software it is in good order.

As a research register it has four problems that matter more than anything
visual, in this order:

1. **The evidence behind the published values no longer exists anywhere.** The
   working database was lost during this audit (section 2).
2. **The monitoring built on 26 October failed on its first scheduled run** and
   nothing told anyone (section 4).
3. **Seven in ten recorded deaths rest on Wikipedia alone**, and a reader can
   check the supporting passage for only 8 of 52 death tolls (section 5).
4. **The register changes character at 2010** and the page compares the two
   halves as if they were alike (section 6).

## 2. What happened during this audit: the working database is gone

The working database (`database/mining_accidents.sqlite`) is excluded from git
by design. It lived in one cloud container. Partway through this audit that
container was replaced, the repository was cloned fresh, and the database and
the 15 MB archive of retrieved source pages under `data/raw/` went with it. A
search of the new container found no copy.

This is open question 22 coming true. It was registered on 12 October, I left
it open across four working sessions while adding to the database, and it
should have been closed before anything else was built on top of it.

**What survives**, because it is committed: the whole public export (52
incident records, 3 environmental records, 258 field citations, 66
classifications, 29 organization roles, 74 sites), the dashboard, and every
script.

**What is lost**, with counts measured on the database earlier the same day:

| Lost | Count |
|---|---|
| Claims | 963, of which 799 never reviewed and 18 awaiting review |
| Reviewer decisions, with rationales and supersession chains | 301 |
| Review log, ingestion runs, casualty observation history | all |
| Archived source pages | 15 MB |
| Evidence added on 29 October: the Kozlu 11 April claim, the EKİ operator candidate, the Armutçuk mechanism claim, the Amasra TBMM and NTV claims with their dated observation, six İSİG monthly aggregates | all |

No exported file carries a decision, a rationale, a reviewer or an observation
date. So the published register can still say *what* each value is and cite a
document for it, but it can no longer show *that a decision selected it* or
*what else was claimed*. For a project whose stated purpose is that every value
is traceable through reviewed claims, that is the central failure.

**Can it be rebuilt?** Not today, and not faithfully without care:

- A dry-run rebuild in a scratch database was attempted three times. The
  Wikidata endpoints answered; `tr.wikipedia.org` answered HTTP 429 to this
  container and the ingest exited with an error.
- The last measured rebuild (12 October) reproduced 42 of 51 records, because
  the upstream list article is edited over time and public identifiers are
  assigned in processing order (open questions 22, 24, 25).
- No rebuild procedure is documented. Eighteen scripts exist, several with
  dates in their names, with no stated order.
- One source the scripts need now refuses this project (Medyascope, HTTP 403).

The live site is unaffected. Nothing published is wrong because of the loss.
What is gone is the ability to change the register correctly: until a database
exists again, no decision can be recorded and no export should be built.

by that one run.

| M3 | **The review session ran before the sweep.** The sweep was scheduled for 06:23 UTC and GitHub started it at 13:39 UTC, more than seven hours late. The review session fired at 08:32 UTC as scheduled, with nothing to review. | Workflow run start time; trigger `last_fired_at` |
| M4 | **The review session had no repository.** It was created without one attached, ran for eight and a half minutes, pushed nothing, and sits unread. | Session tags include `config:no-git-repo` |

which is true, and describes the sweep as monthly, which it has now failed to

the workflow has no failure step and the page has no "last attempted" date.

The İSİG site was reachable from the development container on 26 and
29 October and not from GitHub's runner on 8 November. Whether that is
permanent for GitHub's address ranges is not known from one failure.

**The finding that matters most here is new.** The register holds two different
kinds of record and presents them as one series:

| | Records | Smallest toll | Median toll | Single-fatality records |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Before 2010 | 15 | 3 | 17 | 0 |
| From 2010 | 37 | 1 | 1 | 20 |

Before 2010 only multi-death events reached the sources. From 2010 the list
article records individual deaths. So the series is a record of disasters until
2010 and a record of accidents after it. Any reading across that line compares
unlike things: the count of records rises from 15 to 37 because of what was
written down, not because of what happened.

The page makes that comparison in three places: the four period maps
("so the periods stay comparable"), the four era cards above the year chart,
and the year chart itself. The periods are also of unequal length (17, 10, 10
and 7 years on the map; 17, 14, 3 and 10 on the timeline), and the two sets of
periods differ from each other.

## 10. Decisions that are the owner's

1. **Recovery route.** Restore from the export (P0.1), which is exact for what
   was published and loses the unselected claims for good, or attempt a full
   re-ingest, which may recover more claims and risks renumbering identifiers
   and dropping records. This audit recommends the first, then re-ingesting
   into a separate database to recover unselected claims as evidence only.
2. **Whether the evidence ledger is committed publicly** (P0.2). It holds
   nothing private: documents, quotes already published, and the reviewer name
   already in the commit history.
3. **The "toll status unrecorded" pill** (V5): keep it or remove it.
4. **Province order in the hazards table** (V10): alphabetical, or as it is.
5. **The rates panel** (V22): remove it until an official series exists, or
   keep it trimmed. This audit recommends removing it: the figures are third
   hand, and the owner's own rule of 22 October was to be confident in a
   figure or not show it.

## 11. Limits of this audit

- Checks in section 3 marked as run on the database were run before it was
  lost and cannot be repeated until one exists.
- Evidence verifiability (hash against archived copy), the tier behind each
  decision, and contradicting claims on file were queued and could not be run.
- Accuracy checks against outside sources covered the Zonguldak basin records
  listed by TTK, four date checks against the list article, and the values
  verified in earlier sessions. The other published values were not
  independently re-verified.
- Every panel was read by eye on a desktop. On a phone the header, map, year
  chart, records, sites, rates and environmental panels were read; the
  mechanism, hazards, limits and download panels were measured only. The
  Turkish page was measured and spot-read, not read panel by panel.
- Accessibility was measured, not certified. No screen reader was used.
