# Quality audit, 2026-10-20

Full review of the register as published: the database, the public export, the
dashboard and the pipeline that produces them. Every number below was measured
against the working database or `data/public/` on 2026-10-20, at commit
`9bdd47f` (52 published records, 1,091 recorded deaths, 76 sites, 29 incident
organization roles, 291 decisions, 928 claims).

Findings are ordered by what they cost a reader, not by how hard they are to
fix. Severity means: **A** a reader is misled or loses access to something;
**B** the data is wrong or inconsistent but the page does not mislead;
**C** a limit of the register that is real but already disclosed.

---

## 1. What the register actually is, measured

Three measurements decide how everything else should be read.

**It is a register of disasters, not of mining deaths.** Median deaths per
record is 3, mean is 21, and the three largest records (Soma 2014, Kozlu 1992,
Armutçuk 1983) hold 667 of the 1,091 deaths, 61% of the total. 20 of 52
records are single-fatality.

**Against the only external series the project holds, it captures about a
third.** For 2013-2025 the İSİG series in `aggregate_occupational_statistics`
totals 1,264 deaths; the register documents 430 of them, 34%. In five of those
years (2012, 2017, 2018, 2020, 2021) it documents **none**. The İSİG series
itself checks out: İSİG's own published figure for 2013-2025 is 1,267, three
above the series held here.

**Half the calendar is empty.** 22 of the 44 years from 1983 to 2026 carry no
record at all: 1984-1986, 1988-1989, 1991, 1993-1994, 1996-2002, 2007-2008,
2012, 2017-2018, 2020-2021. Türkiye did not have accident-free years; these are
gaps in what has been documented here.

Where the register *is* strong: large underground coal disasters, 1983-2014.
It holds 21 records of five or more deaths totalling 980 deaths for that
period, against a figure quoted in the Turkish mining literature of 19
underground-coal disasters and 944 deaths for the same span. Large-disaster
coverage is close to complete; everything else is thin.

**Consequence for every chart on the page.** The severity profile in the
hazard matrix, the province distribution, the per-year bars and the operator
breakdown are all computed over a set that systematically over-represents
catastrophes. None of them is wrong about what it documents; all of them would
look different if the single-fatality majority were present. The page says the
register is partial. It does not yet say that the partiality has a *shape*,
and the shape is the thing a reader needs.

## 2. Severity A: a reader is misled or loses something

### A1. Four published identifiers now resolve to nothing

*Fixed 2026-10-20: redirects are derived from the merge log's incident ids and
the records' own publication status instead of the mis-stamped column, and all
four now resolve in `merged_id_redirects.csv`.*

`merged_id_redirects.csv` is empty, yet four records that were assigned public
identifiers have been withdrawn after merges: `TR-MINE-1992-0002`,
`TR-MINE-2014-0014`, `TR-MINE-2014-0015`, `TR-MINE-2016-0002`. Anyone holding
a citation to one of those gets nothing back.

The cause is a stamping bug, not a missing feature. Every row in
`incident_merge_log` carries the **survivor's** public id in
`merged_public_incident_id`, the column meant for the id that was merged away.
The export joins that column to the survivor, so all four rows come out as
self-redirects and the self-redirect filter in `_collect_redirects` removes
them. The filter is right; the data it reads is wrong.

Fix: stamp the merged record's id. `incident_merge_log` is append-only, so the
correction is new rows plus an export that prefers the latest row per pair, or
a redirect table derived from `incidents.publication_status = 'withdrawn'`
joined to the merge log by incident id rather than by public id.

### A2. The merge log records both directions for the same merge

*Mitigated 2026-10-20: the log still holds both directions and still cannot be
edited, but no consumer reads direction from it any more. The export decides
direction from which record is withdrawn and which is published, so the
ambiguity no longer reaches anyone. A superseding marker on the log itself
remains open.*

Rows 1-3 state that the records now published (ids 10, 6, 7) were merged
*into* the records now withdrawn (41, 28, 13). Rows 4-7 state the reverse.
Both sets are in the log, which is append-only, so neither can be removed. A
reader of the provenance trail cannot tell which merge happened; a tool reading
it will believe whichever it reads last.

This is residue from the merge-direction error corrected on 2026-10-14. It was
correct not to delete the rows. It is not correct to leave the log ambiguous:
it needs a superseding marker, the way `claim_decisions` has
`supersedes_decision_id`.

### A3. "Documented sites now" cannot say "now"

*Fixed 2026-10-20: every "now" is gone from the site counts, and the province
table's caption states that operational status is unrecorded for all 76 sites,
so these are sites the register holds rather than sites working today.*

`operational_status` is `unknown` for **all 76** sites. The hazard matrix
column is headed "Documented sites now" and the province tooltip says "5
documented mining site(s)". Nothing in the data supports the present tense: a
site in the layer may have closed decades ago. Etibank, broken up in 2004,
still appears as owner on 16 historic site records (open question 28).

Fix: either source operational status, or re-label the column to "sites
documented in this register" and drop "now" everywhere, including
`prov_sites`, `site_coverage` and the matrix heading.

### A4. The deaths-per-output comparison is not a comparison

`rate_context` feeds a panel headed "Historical deaths-per-output figures" with
six rows: Türkiye 2000 (710) and 2008 (722), China 2008 (127) and 2013 (37),
and the United States **with no year at all** (1 and 6). The undated rows sat
in the same table as the dated ones (it is a table, not a chart: an earlier
draft of this audit said "plotted", which was wrong), the years do not align
across countries, and the provenance is "press figures via the Wikipedia list
article". A reader takes
away "Türkiye is 700x the United States", which these numbers cannot support.

The caption discloses the limits and open question 18 tracks the fix, but
disclosure does not repair a comparison in which some rows have no year.

*Fixed 2026-10-20: the two undated figures are kept but moved below the dated
table under their own heading, which states that a figure without a year
cannot be compared with one that has a year. The panel still awaits a
TÜİK/TKİ-denominated series (open question 18).*

### A5. Hardcoded counts in the About box will go stale

*Fixed 2026-10-20: both counts, and the environmental scope count added since,
are computed from the payload at render time.*

`ab8_d` states "24 of 52 records name one" in English and "52 kaydın 24'ünde"
in Turkish, written into the i18n strings. The next record or role changes the
truth of a sentence that no build step checks. Every other count on the page is
computed from the payload; these two are not.

## 3. Severity B: wrong or inconsistent data

### B1. A published toll conflicts with an unreviewed claim on the same record

`TR-MINE-2010-0004` (Odaköy, Balıkesir) publishes 17 deaths. Claim 117, from
the incident's own Wikipedia article, says 13 and sits unreviewed. The article
narrates 13 deaths rising to 14; the list article's bullet says 17; the same
pit's 2006 accident, published separately as `TR-MINE-2006-0001`, also says 17.
Two accidents at one pit with an identical toll is the shape of a transcription
error. Open question 31 records this. It is still open and the register still
publishes the higher figure with no `casualty_status` marking it disputed.

Minimum fix: set `casualty_status = 'disputed'` so the page shows the pill it
already has for Amasra.

### B2. A toll conflict nobody has logged yet

`TR-MINE-1995-0001` (Sorgun, Yozgat) publishes **38** deaths from the Wikipedia
list. The Anadolu Ajansı chronology republished by Cumhuriyet gives **37** for
the same accident. Neither source is primary. The record carries no
`casualty_status`.

### B3. Eleven ai-assisted claims still await review, four of them on fatalities

Claims 78, 106, 117, 827 are fatality claims; 819-824 are the whole claim set
for the 2026 Soma record, which is published from them by decision but whose
claims were never individually reviewed. Constraint 5 is not violated, because the
decisions cite reviewed claims, but the review queue has been carried for
weeks and includes the contradiction in B1.

### B4. Commodity coding splits the same commodity across codes and languages

*Fixed 2026-10-20: six codes added to the vocabulary (bauxite, cobalt,
manganese, nickel, platinum_group, salt), 11 sites recoded off `other`, 38
labels normalised to one language, and the page now renders a commodity from
its code so each reader sees one language. No site is coded `other` any more.*

Labels mix Turkish and English for one code: `coal`/`kömür` (19 and 4),
`copper`/`bakır` (4 and 2), `chromium`/`krom` (2 and 1). Eleven sites with
specific commodities are binned as `other` while carrying the real name in the
label: bauxite, cobalt ore, **gold ore**, manganese, nickel ore, palladium,
phosphates, rock salt, silver ore. Gold is therefore split between the `gold`
code (7 sites) and an `other` row labelled "gold ore"; phosphate between
`phosphate`/`fosfat` (1) and `other`/`phosphates` (2). The dashboard's
commodity filter and the site breakdown both inherit the split. 14 sites have
no commodity at all.

### B5. Six sites share coordinates with another site

Afşin-C, Afşin-D, Afşin-E and Kışlaköy all sit on 38.340685, 37.082979; Eynez
kömür ocağı and Soma Kömür Madeni both on 39.077194, 27.525258. QC flags both
as likely source copy errors. They are unresolved, and the second pair sits
exactly where the register's largest accident is mapped.

### B6. The duplicate-candidate check cannot clear

Two of the fifteen QC warnings are duplicate candidates (province 56 at
similarity 1.0, province 74 at 0.90). Both pairs were merged and the losing
record withdrawn. The check does not exclude withdrawn records, so it will warn
forever, which trains the reader to ignore it.

### B7. Two records were dropped without resolution

`Nesko Şebinkarahisar Maden İşletmesi felaketi` (2021-11-18) is marked
out of scope with the rationale "no reported fatalities (tailings-pond
collapse)". `Maden kazası, Muğla (2022-03-01)` is `scope_undetermined` and
withdrawn, with no rationale beyond its seed. Neither appears anywhere public.
The first is a correct scope call under a fatal-accidents definition, and is
also the clearest evidence that the register has no environmental track at all
(see D3). The second is simply unfinished.

### B8. Four records remain at month precision, and two have a known day

`TR-MINE-1987-0001` (Kozlu, 8 deaths) is stored as January 1987; the AA
chronology gives **31 January 1987**. `TR-MINE-2006-0001` (Dursunbey, 17
deaths) is stored as 1 June 2006 at exact precision; AA gives **2 June 2006**,
so that one is a conflict rather than a sharpening. `TR-MINE-1983-0003` (July
1983) and `TR-MINE-2011-0001` (February 2011) remain month-only.

### B9. One identifier no longer matches its own record

`TR-MINE-2024-0004` now carries 23 November 2023 after the correction of
2026-10-16. Keeping the identifier was deliberate and is documented in open
question 32, but nothing on the page explains why a record dated 2023 has a
2024 id, and a reader sorting by id will mis-order it.

## 4. Severity C: real limits, already disclosed

- **26 of 52 records rest on a single document**, and 50 of the 53 documents
  backing published claims are Tier 3. Only two Tier 1 documents (TBMM) and one
  Tier 2 (the chamber bulletin added on 2026-10-16) exist. Open question 12.
- **One incident in 52 is linked to a facility.** Every site-level analysis is
  therefore impossible, including the one most worth doing (see E1).
- **No district, no settlement, on any record.** Both columns are empty for all
  52, though sources routinely give them ("Bartın'ın Amasra ilçesinde",
  "Dursunbey ilçesine bağlı Odaköy").
- **Injuries recorded on 5 of 52 records; missing persons on none.** The human
  cost beyond deaths is essentially absent.
- **14 records carry no event mechanism and 29 no hazard**, so the hazard
  matrix's uncoded row is a sixth of the register and its hazard axis covers
  under half.
- **`aggregate_employment`, `aggregate_production`, `aggregate_licence_context`,
  `recommendations`, `classification_concordance`, `facility_aliases` and
  `organization_aliases` are all empty tables.** The schema anticipates
  denominators, official recommendations and alias handling; none exist.

## 5. Coverage gaps, by kind

### D1. Temporal
The 22 empty years listed in section 1. The worst stretch is 1996-2002, seven
consecutive years, in a period when TTK's own statistics record 4, 9, 5 and 8
deaths for 1999-2002 alone.

### D2. Severity
Single-fatality and two-to-four-fatality accidents, which are the majority of
mining deaths in Türkiye and about 38% of this register. İSİG's mining report
counts 1,267 deaths across 2013-2025 and states that 70% fall into two
categories, collapse/crushing and poisoning/suffocation, mostly small events
that this register does not hold.

### D3. Sectoral and environmental
- **Quarries and marble**: 6 quarry sites and 3 archaeological quarries in the
  sites layer; **zero** quarry accidents among the 52 records, though quarrying
  kills workers every year.
- **Metal and industrial minerals**: three records only (Küre copper 2004,
  Şirvan copper 2016/2023, Çöpler gold 2024).
- **Environmental harm was entirely out of scope and nowhere stated.**
  *Resolved on 2026-10-20 by the project owner's scope extension: see
  `docs/environmental_scope_protocol.md` and open question 34. Three records
  now publish with `TR-ENV` identifiers, counted separately from the deaths.
  The paragraph below records the position this audit found.* The İliç
  2024 record exists because nine people died; that it was a cyanide heap-leach
  failure whose spill reached the Euphrates basin is recorded nowhere. The
  Şebinkarahisar tailings-dam collapse was excluded for having no deaths. A
  reader cannot tell whether the absence of environmental incidents is a scope
  decision or a gap. It is a scope decision, and it is undocumented.

### D4. Known missing incidents
At least one multi-fatality event is documented in the press and absent here:
**Şırnak, 17 October 2017, seven workers killed** in a landslide at an
illegally operated coal mine. It falls in one of the empty years. A systematic
sweep of 2015-2026 against İSİG's monthly bulletins would almost certainly find
dozens more.

## 6. What the data can already answer, and does not

These need no new sources. They are the analyses a reader would most want.

### E1. Repeat accidents at the same site and the same company
Already visible without the facility link:
- **Türkiye Taşkömürü Kurumu: 9 records, 466 deaths, 1983 to 2024.** The same
  state operator, largely the same basin, across 41 years.
- **Şentaş Madencilik: 2 records at one pit** (Odaköy, Dursunbey), 2006 and
  2010, 34 deaths. A second fatal accident at a pit that had already had one.

"Had this site already had a documented accident?" is the single most
prevention-relevant question the register can ask, and answering it properly
needs only `incidents.facility_id`, which is populated for 1 record in 52.

### E2. Operator type, stated without a league table
Documented deaths split almost evenly: 473 under a private operator (13
records), 466 under a state enterprise (9 records), 87 under an operator whose
type is unknown, and 65 in the 28 records naming no company. The even split
refuses both of the easy narratives, which is exactly why it is worth
publishing: with the denominators absent, it describes the record, not risk.

### E3. Subcontracting
Three records name a subcontractor: Küre 2004 (19 dead), Karadon 2010 (30),
Şirvan 2016 (16). All three are multi-fatality; all three are among the larger
records. Three cases cannot establish a pattern, but subcontracting is a
documented risk factor in the literature and the register currently captures it
only where a source volunteers it.

### E4. Seasonality, with a warning attached
By record count the year is fairly flat (November 8, June 7, January and
October 5 each, December 1). By deaths it is dominated by two months, March
(405) and May (332), which is Kozlu 1992 and Soma 2014 and nothing else. Any
seasonality claim from 52 records is noise; the honest version is the record
count, shown with the caveat.

### E5. Time between accidents, and time to recovery of the dead
Both computable in part, neither recorded. The interval between accidents at
one operator is derivable now. Time to recovery (five years at Kozlu 1992) is
in the sources but has no column.

## 7. What data to add, in order of value

1. **İSİG Meclisi monthly bulletins as an incident-level source.** The single
   highest-value addition: it is the only series that would close the 66% gap
   for 2013 onward, and the register already uses its annual totals. The
   bulletins publish dates, places and counts monthly. Two obstacles, both
   solvable: they are distributed as images, so extraction is `ocr_assisted`
   and every claim must pass human review (constraint 5, which the pipeline
   already enforces); and they name the dead, so extraction must take place,
   date and count only and never the name (the privacy protocol already
   requires this).
2. **SGK work-accident statistics.** Official, annual, by sector and province,
   and the only realistic route to **employment denominators**, without which
   no rate on this page can be honest. Tier 1.
3. **TÜİK and TKİ production series.** Denominators for deaths-per-output,
   which would replace the broken comparison in A4. Open question 18.
4. **TBMM investigation commission reports** for Soma 2014 and Amasra 2022.
   Tier 1, and the natural source for the empty `recommendations` table: what
   was recommended after each disaster, and whether it was implemented, is the
   most direct "help people avoid more" content the project could carry.
5. **TTK annual reports.** Authoritative for the Zonguldak basin, where 11 of
   52 records sit, and a per-year death series back to 1983 that would fill
   much of the empty calendar for one basin.
6. **TMMOB Chamber of Mining Engineers accident bulletins.** Now assessed and
   openly served; they publish a bulletin per significant accident with
   technical detail no press report carries.
7. **MAPEG licence register** for the 28 records naming no company and for
   licence holders generally. Still blocked by the environment's network
   policy.
8. **Two new per-incident variables**, both decisive for hazard analysis and
   both usually stated in sources: **underground or surface**, and **employment
   status of the dead** (direct employee, subcontracted, informal). Neither
   exists in the schema today.

## 8. Suggested order of work

**First, because a reader is currently misled or blocked:**
A1 redirects, A3 "now", A4 the rate panel, A5 hardcoded counts, B1 the Odaköy
toll marked disputed.

**Second, because they are cheap and the data is already in hand:**
B2 and B8 (toll and date conflicts from the AA chronology), B4 commodity
coding, B6 the duplicate check, district and settlement for the records whose
sources state them, the empty `casualty_status` column generally.

**Third, the structural ones:**
`incidents.facility_id` for as many records as the sources allow, which unlocks
E1; the underground/surface flag; clearing the 11-claim review queue; A2 the
merge log.

**Fourth, the sources, in the order of section 7**, starting with İSİG, which
is worth more than everything else on this list combined.

**Separately, a scope decision for the project owner:** whether this register
documents fatal accidents only, or also environmental harm from mining.
*Decided 2026-10-20: it covers both, under `docs/environmental_scope_protocol.md`
(STATUS: PROPOSED, open question 34). Environmental records carry their own
identifiers and counts and never enter a death toll.*

## 9. What is working

Worth stating, because an audit that lists only faults is not an honest one.

- **The evidence chain holds.** Every one of the 52 published records has an
  active decision for every canonical field checked; no ai-assisted claim has
  become canonical without review; 291 decisions, 924 logged review actions.
- **The export validates clean**: 0 errors against the datapackage schema, 0
  critical quality findings.
- **The İSİG comparison series is accurate** to within 3 deaths of İSİG's own
  published total for 2013-2025.
- **Large-disaster coverage for 1983-2014 is close to complete** against the
  figure quoted in the literature.
- **The organization roles are honest work**: 29 roles across 24 records, every
  one quoting a fetched document whose hash is stored, with the one
  union-sourced attribution carried as `alleged` rather than `reported`.
- **The page does not overclaim.** The limits box, the hazard-matrix method
  note and the open-questions register say most of what this audit says about
  scope. What they do not yet say is the *shape* of the partiality, which is
  section 1 and is the most important single sentence this project could add.
